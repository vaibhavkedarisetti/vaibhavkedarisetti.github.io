Teardowns

Seven lenses I use to take a product apart, and the question each one is really asking.

Most teardowns are screenshots with opinions attached. The useful version starts from a mechanism: what is this product trying to make happen, what does it cost the user to comply, and where does the loop break when it breaks.

The seven lenses Interactive

Lens 01

Onboarding

How many steps stand between arriving and the first moment that is actually worth something? Count them, then ask what each one is protecting. Most exist to make a database tidy rather than to make a user successful.

Tell: a product that asks for commitment before it has demonstrated value is not confident in the value.

Lens 02

Discovery

Who decides what a user sees first: the user, an algorithm, or an editor? That choice determines what the product optimises for and who it quietly disadvantages. Look at the empty state: it is where a team’s real assumptions about the user are visible.

Tell: if search is the primary navigation, discovery has been outsourced to the user.

Lens 03

Core loop

What action does the product need repeated, and what does it give back to make repetition worth it? A loop that only pays off for the company is a funnel wearing a costume. Name the trigger, the action, the reward and the investment. If one is missing, the retention numbers will explain why.

Tell: if you cannot state the loop in one sentence, the product does not have one yet.

Lens 04

Retention

Does the product get more valuable the longer you use it, or merely harder to leave? Accumulated value and accumulated lock-in look identical on a retention curve and are completely different products. Check what a user would lose by leaving, and whether they built it or the company did.

Tell: heavy notification dependence usually means the loop is not self-sustaining.

Lens 05

Monetisation

What is priced, and does the price rise with the value the user gets or with the cost the company bears? Look at what sits behind the paywall: if it is a limit rather than a capability, the pricing is taxing the loop instead of extending it.

Tell: free tiers that remove the core loop do not convert, they filter.

Lens 06

UX

Where does the interface make a decision on the user’s behalf, and where does it refuse to? Two equally weighted buttons is a team that could not agree. Defaults are the strongest opinion a product holds, and the one users notice least.

Tell: count the primary actions per screen. More than one means none.

Lens 07

Growth

Does using the product create a reason for someone else to use it, or does growth have to be bought every month? Distinguish a genuine loop from a referral programme bolted onto one. And check whether growth degrades the core experience. Most social products die of their own acquisition.

Tell: if the growth mechanic only works for power users, it is a feature, not a loop.

Published

Teardowns in progress

I am not publishing teardowns of products I have not used properly, and I am not publishing claims about their internal metrics that I cannot source. The lenses above are the method; the write-ups follow when they are worth reading.

Standard

One lens leads and the others check it. A teardown that touches all seven equally has not found anything.
