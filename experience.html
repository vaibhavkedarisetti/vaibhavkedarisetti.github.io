Practice

What I actually do

I find a problem, check whether it is real by talking to people who have it, decide what not to build, ship the smallest honest version, instrument it, and read what comes back. Then I do it again with better information.

The technical half is not decoration. Understanding how the thing gets built is what lets me argue about scope credibly, estimate what a trade-off actually costs, and write requirements an engineer does not have to translate.

Technical foundation

Enterprise work taught me how software actually ships

Requirements decay between the workshop and the ticket. Integrations fail at the seams rather than in the middle. A surprising amount of what a product becomes is decided by constraints nobody wrote down, usually late, and usually by whoever sits closest to the code.

That is the half of product management you cannot get from a framework, and it is why I can argue about scope with engineers rather than at them.

None of it is the point of this site. It is the reason I can be trusted with the rest of it.
